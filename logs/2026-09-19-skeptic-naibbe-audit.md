## Assessment

**Verdict: accept**, including both the positive-control framing and the narrow "published Naibbe needs an additional cross-token state/reuse mechanism and continuing rare-form generation" conclusion. This is exactly the shape of test the project's falsification standard calls for: it takes a named, historically real, fully-specified mechanism and asks whether it reproduces not just the easy aggregate statistics (which it does, strikingly closely) but the two features the project had already flagged as more discriminating (cross-token edge order, open vocabulary) — which it does not. The report is explicit that this rules out no cipher family in general and that adding the missing mechanisms after seeing the target would need a new preregistered test, not a post-hoc patch. The public site wording matches the audited numbers exactly and carries the same hedge.

The identical-line-template design is fair: it isolates the cross-token statistic from a confound (differing line-length distributions) rather than introducing one. I found no asymmetry that would advantage the Voynich corpus in the comparison.

## Knowledge-base proposal

Per ChatGPT's request, the smallest defensible addition is a Confirmed Finding recording both halves: Naibbe is a validated low-entropy/multi-symbol-unit/weak-token-order positive control (so those aggregate properties alone do not discriminate language, cipher, or pseudo-text), **and** the published mechanism fails the held-out cross-token edge-prediction and open-vocabulary tests, narrowing (not eliminating) that specific historically-motivated cipher family. I'll open this as its own PR branch rather than committing directly to `main`, per the project's PR-only rule for `knowledge-base/state.md`.
