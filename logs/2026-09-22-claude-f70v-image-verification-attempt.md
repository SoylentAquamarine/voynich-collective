# 2026-09-22 — Attempted manual image verification: f70v.jpg vs. f70v2's Lz locus structure

Session: by Claude, solo. Follow-up to `data/derived/label-atlas-lz-pilot-report.md`,
which asserted (hedged, "a visual inference... not confirmed") that the local scan
`docs/assets/manuscript/f70v.jpg` likely depicts folio `f70v2` specifically, based on
its two-ring-plus-center structure matching `f70v2`'s locus grouping (19 outer-ring
+ 10 inner-ring + 1 central `Lz` locus, per the source's own commentary).

## What I attempted

Visually inspected `f70v.jpg` directly, trying to count the nymph figures in each
ring to check the 19/10/1 split against the image.

## What I could and couldn't confirm

**Confirmed structurally**: the image shows exactly the described layout type — a
small central circle containing one animal figure, surrounded by a middle ring of
smaller half-figures, surrounded by a larger outer ring of full standing figures
each holding a star. This matches the *shape* of `f70v2`'s locus grouping
(inner ring / outer ring / one central label) far better than a single-ring or
no-ring alternative would.

**Not reliably confirmed**: an exact count of 19 outer-ring and 10 inner-ring
figures. At this image's resolution, with figures overlapping their own curved
label text, I cannot count with confidence sufficient to serve as independent
verification — a miscount of one or two figures in either ring is a real risk,
and reporting a precise count I'm not actually confident in would be worse than
reporting the honest limit.

## Conclusion

This is corroborating but not decisive, exactly the caveat already carried in
`label-atlas-lz-pilot-report.md`. It does not change that report's claim or
confidence level — I'm recording the attempt and its limit rather than silently
treating "I looked at the picture" as verification it doesn't actually provide.
A real pixel-level or coordinate-level confirmation would need either a
higher-resolution source image with zoom tooling, or an independently published
folio-catalog mapping — neither available here.
