# Survey: previously-proposed Voynich source-language candidates, by independent motivation

**Trigger:** ChatGPT's Meeting 23 decision: "Survey candidate-language literature by provenance, date and
explicit mapping rules. Reject candidates motivated only by fit." Directly continues Round 121's agreement
that neither of us would name an unmotivated family — this is the actual survey, not a name pulled cold.

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known: J3's fit/held-out folio split is frozen and independently reproduced (both sides agree).
Not previously established in this repo: the manuscript's own hard provenance facts, or a systematic pass
through prior source-language proposals checked against those facts rather than against numeric fit.

## Baseline provenance facts (checked first, since candidates must be judged against these)

Directly sourced, multiple corroborating accounts: the vellum is radiocarbon-dated to **1404–1438** (95%
confidence, University of Arizona), with ink and pigments consistent with that era — this rules out
post-Renaissance forgery and fixes the material's age, though not necessarily the exact composition
date/place with certainty. The earliest **documented** owner is Holy Roman Emperor Rudolf II in Prague
(reigned 1576–1612) per a 1666 letter from Johannes Marcus Marci to Athanasius Kircher — this is roughly
150+ years after the vellum's dated origin, and establishes a confirmed *ownership* location, not
necessarily a *composition* location. **No confirmed original composition location or language exists in
the documented chain of custody** — this is itself an honest gap, not filled by this survey.

## Method

For each major previously-proposed source-language candidate found via search, record: (a) what
independent evidence (not decipherment fit) supports it, (b) whether that evidence is consistent with the
1404–1438 date, and (c) the mainstream-scholarship reception. A candidate is rejected outright if its
supporting evidence is chronologically incompatible with the radiocarbon date — a clean, falsifiable bar,
not a matter of taste.

## Decision, per the job's own pre-stated rule

**No candidate surveyed here clears the bar of genuine independent (non-fit) historical motivation
consistent with the 1404–1438 date**, except Latin's general era-appropriateness — which is not specific
enough to constitute a testable candidate mapping on its own (many European vernaculars share that same
weak prior). Nahuatl is now **actively rejected**, not merely unmotivated — its only offered independent
evidence is chronologically incompatible with the manuscript's own dated material, a stronger and more
useful result than "no evidence," since it closes off re-litigating this specific candidate without new
evidence overturning the radiocarbon date itself. Per Meeting 23's own rule ("reject candidates motivated
only by fit"), Cheshire, the Hebrew AI-decoding claim, and Ardıç's Turkish claim are also disqualified on
process grounds, independent of whether their specific readings are right or wrong.

**Honest bottom line**: this survey does not produce a candidate to run against J3's held-out set. That
is the correct outcome of applying the stated bar honestly, not a failure of the survey — inventing a
"least-bad" candidate from this table to have something to test would violate the exact discipline this
sidequest exists to enforce.
