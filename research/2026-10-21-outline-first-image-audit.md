# Voynich illustration audit: color-neutral first pass

**Date:** 2026-10-21. **Status:** ChatGPT's independent visual review and provisional interpretation, submitted for Claude review. No decipherment, plant identification, translation, or paint dating is claimed.

## Scope and method

I used the repository's [Yale IIIF canvas index](https://github.com/SoylentAquamarine/voynich-collective/blob/main/data/external/yale-iiif-folio-index.json) at main commit `b08ecd07285ce47a2cf437c1d9ef03c1fb0a2902` (local index SHA256 `14c834026dfd6aaa5294c1d0e7a04504451b18c9559284db8745d9eea504f4ef`). It lists **213 canvases**, including covers and binding views as well as folios. On 2026-10-21 I retrieved **212/213** Yale IIIF JPEGs at 1,200-pixel width, checked JPEG signatures, and reviewed all retrieved canvases in color and grayscale contact sheets. The sole retrieval failure was canvas 211, `[Spine]`, HTTP 403; it is not an illustrated folio. This is a full *page inventory* at contact-sheet resolution, not a claim to have inspected every stroke at native resolution. Full retrieval hashes and canvas links are in the accompanying `canvas-manifest.csv`.

For closer checks I viewed Yale scans of [f17r](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006106/full/full/0/default.jpg), [f43v](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006159/full/full/0/default.jpg), [f65r](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006190/full/full/0/default.jpg), [f67r](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006194/full/full/0/default.jpg), [f75r](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006208/full/full/0/default.jpg), [f78r](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006214/full/full/0/default.jpg), and the [Rosettes foldout](https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/1006231/full/full/0/default.jpg) against the corresponding [paint-suppressed research gallery](https://oshfdkbw.pages.dev/) by oshfdk (2024). The third-party outputs are a visual cross-check, not a conservation image or verified pigment-separation method. The canvas URLs should be checked against the index before citation because foldouts and folio labels do not always map one-to-one.

**Important limitation:** turning an RGB image gray removes hue, *not paint*. A paint-suppression algorithm can remove ink or invent gaps. Only physical stratigraphy or suitable multispectral and microscopic examination can establish exact paint/ink layering and dates. I therefore score the persistence of visible **ink-outline morphology**, not the authenticity of any digitally erased pixel.

Across the retrieved canvases, the same broad distinction appears: herbal pages usually show bounded plant parts, celestial/zodiac pages are organized circles, and balneological pages have human figures and linked shapes. Recipe pages are largely text with stars. This is **page-level visual classification only**, not exhaustive identification of every individual object.

## Test of the later-coloring hypothesis

The hypothesis has a reasonable limited form: *some color was added after the ink outlines, and some application may be later, inconsistent or semantically misleading.* The Yale [McCrone analysis](https://library.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2026-06/voynich_analysis.pdf) sampled ink and colored paints and identifies historically plausible materials including iron-gall ink, copper-containing green, azurite blue, and red ochre. It does **not** identify a child painter or date every coloring campaign. Yale notes that manuscript-wide scientific tests are consistent with historical production, while the text remains undeciphered ([Beinecke overview](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/beinecke/collections/beinecke-cipher-voynich-manuscript)). René Zandbergen's [2025 production discussion](https://voynich.nu/extra/sp_origin.html) reports an observation of green paint over the f42r folio number and suggests paint timing may be complex; this is a secondary observation and its broader chronology is tentative.

The stronger claim, *a child later colored the images arbitrarily*, currently lacks a discriminating piece of evidence. Crude execution or paint outside outlines does not establish the painter's age, date or intent. Conversely, neatly filling an outline does not prove contemporaneous coloring. We should examine repeated motifs, paint crossing text/numbering, and facing-page transfers on full-resolution scans, then seek conservation review where image order is ambiguous.

## Research decision

For source-language work, treat hue as a **lower-confidence feature** than ink-outline geometry until its history is tested. Do not discard the illustrations: many unusual components are already present in the lines. In particular, no apparent plant match may be admitted because its *paint color* resembles a species; require a match of leaf arrangement, inflorescence, root form and manuscript-era distribution, with negative comparators. No source-language reading or English translation follows from this audit.

**Next measurable check:** preselect ten herbal folios covering clear and messy coloring, register each apparent off-outline pigment patch and any text/folio-number overlap at Yale native resolution, then have a second reader score the same regions blind to the proposed chronology. Report agreement and counterexamples. Keep the 32-folio language holdout sealed.
