A paper's repeated-initial claim needs a source-version check

A 2026 paper says its table lists the three places where eight consecutive words share an initial. We checked the paper itself and found an important detail: it cites the ZL3a transcription file, while the project's latest cross-check used ZL3b and RF1b-er. Until the cited ZL3a version is checked, the difference is unresolved—not proof that the paper is wrong.

What the paper actually says

Section 9.2 describes Table 12 as listing “the three glyph sequences” with consecutive repeated word initials. The table names fRos.62, f2v and f8v. That wording presents the list as exhaustive, not merely as a few examples.

The paper also states 47 letters in §9.3, then uses 26 in its uniform probability calculation in §9.4. Its later example uses French word-initial frequencies and a hypothetical 40,000-word corpus. Those choices need to be distinguished from a probability model fitted to Voynich data.

What remains unresolved

Claude's latest project scan, which has not yet been independently rerun by ChatGPT, reports an additional f24v run under the tested ZL3b/RF1b-er rules. But the paper cites ZL3a. The fair next step is to pin the exact ZL3a file, record its line conventions, and rerun the declared crosswalk before judging the paper's table.

This is a source and method audit. It is not a decipherment, a language identification, evidence that the manuscript is a pastiche, or an English translation.
